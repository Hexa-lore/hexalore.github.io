It’s a random Wednesday and I’m still getting the same migraines I used to get when I was in college. It got me thinking—some things really don’t change. Or maybe I still don’t know how to take care of myself.

Anyway, that’s not really the point of this entry.

I just realized that a lot has already happened since 2026 began. I lost my grandpa in January, and things have felt a little empty since then. Honestly, I’m glad we were still able to celebrate his birthday before he passed. I miss him.

This year has made me realize how many things I take for granted. I started thinking about the difference between my old self and who I am now. I realized that lately, I’ve been focusing too much on what I should be right now instead of appreciating what I already have and what I’m capable of doing.

I’ve been thinking about how far along I am in life, when in reality, I already have so many great experiences to share. People often say that I’m full of stories and lore they never expected me to have—and honestly, they’re right.

What I really want to say is that I fell into a dark headspace for a while. Stress from work and personal life started getting out of hand. I couldn’t keep up with the same energy anymore, and I started burning out faster than I expected.

But today I took some time to reflect. I realized that alongside the sad moments, there were also a lot of good ones.

My first steak of the year was at Forest Café, sponsored (of course) by my boyfriend.
Our first date of the year was at the Philippine Arena, where we also ate steak and tried some fun activities.
The first horror movie I watched this year was Send Help—I’d rate it a 2.5/5.
The first animated movie of the year was Hoppers, which is a solid 5/5. When we watched it, my boyfriend also invited my siblings and cousins.
My first volunteer work of the year was planting mangroves in Malolos, Bulacan.
My first project of the year was a mini-game I created for my boyfriend.
My first travel plan of the year is preparing for Taiwan.
The first big home improvement this year was tiling and renovating the kitchen.

I am blessed to have these experiences. I am blessed to be able to live through them.

I hope I keep remembering to acknowledge what I experience day by day—to not take things for granted.

Burnout is real. Sometimes you have to pause. Take a step back and see how good the world can be in the little things we experience—from what we eat, to what we watch, listen to, and do.

Be intentional with each step. It doesn’t matter how fast you move.

Just be at peace with yourself.
Don’t compare your path to others.
